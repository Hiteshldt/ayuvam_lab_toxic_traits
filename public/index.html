How it works

In one breath

Each creature is a real government air-quality monitoring station, standing where it stands on the map. The higher it floats, the dirtier the air. Its colour is the official CPCB band, the glow on the ground is the pollution around it, and the worst ones have taken up smoking.

What's real

  • AQI comes from the Central Pollution Control Board's live feed on data.gov.in. For each station it's the higher of the PM2.5 and PM10 sub-indices, averaged over 24 hours. The official AQI also counts gases, so treat this as the particulate part of the story. If CPCB is offline, the site switches to modelled readings from Open-Meteo at the same spots and says so.
  • Cigarettes use Berkeley Earth's rule of thumb: breathing 22 µg/m³ of PM2.5 for a day is roughly as bad as smoking one cigarette. It's a comparison, not a diagnosis.
  • The personalities are jokes. A small AI model writes them once an hour from the numbers, or they come from a hand-written list. Only the air gets roasted, never the people breathing it.
